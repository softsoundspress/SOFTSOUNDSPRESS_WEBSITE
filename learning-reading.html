LEARNING & READING

Building Confidence Through Reading

Reading develops over time. Simple language, repetition, visual support, familiar words, and encouragement can help children build early literacy skills at a pace that works for them.

Start With Familiar Words

Children may connect more easily with words they already hear and use in everyday life.

Begin with familiar people, foods, toys, animals, actions, and routines.

Use Repetition

Repetition can help children recognize patterns, remember words, and build confidence.

Reading the same sentence pattern several times can make new language feel more predictable.

"I see a dog."
"I see a cat."
"I see a ball."

Pair Words With Pictures

Pictures can help children connect written or spoken words with meaning.

Use clear images that directly match the word or sentence being taught whenever possible.

Keep Sentences Simple

Short sentences can reduce language demands while helping children focus on the key words.

"She runs."
"He eats."
"I like apples."

Follow the Child's Interests

Motivation matters. If a child loves trains, animals, music, vehicles, or favorite characters, use those interests during reading activities.

Interest can make learning feel meaningful rather than like a task.

Give Time to Process

Some children need extra time to look at a picture, recognize a word, understand a question, or respond.

Avoid rushing. Give the child time to think before offering the answer.

Read Together

Reading does not always have to mean a child reads every word independently.

Take turns, point to pictures, finish familiar sentences, make sounds, identify objects, or simply enjoy the story together.

Celebrate Progress

Progress may look different for every child.

Recognizing one new word, matching a picture correctly, completing a sentence, or choosing to sit with a book can all be meaningful steps forward.

READING CAN GROW ONE STEP AT A TIME

Confidence Comes Before Complexity

Children do not need to master everything at once. Predictable patterns, meaningful words, encouragement, and repeated success can help build a strong foundation for reading.
