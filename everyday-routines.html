EVERYDAY ROUTINES

Creating Predictability and Confidence

Daily routines can help children know what to expect, prepare for transitions, build independence, and feel more secure throughout the day.

Keep Routines Predictable

When possible, keep common parts of the day in a familiar order.

Predictability can reduce uncertainty and help children understand what is happening next.

Use Visual Schedules

Pictures, symbols, or simple written schedules can help make the day more understandable.

Breakfast → Get Dressed → School → Home → Dinner → Bed

Use First-Then Language

First-then language can make expectations clearer and help children understand what comes next.

"First shoes, then outside."

"First bath, then bedtime story."

Prepare for Transitions

Moving from one activity to another can be difficult, especially when a child is deeply engaged in something they enjoy.

Give advance notice when possible: five minutes, two minutes, then the transition.

Break Tasks Into Smaller Steps

A large task may feel overwhelming. Breaking it into smaller steps can make it easier to understand.

Get dressed: shirt → pants → socks → shoes.

Give Choices When Possible

Small choices can help children feel more involved while still keeping the routine moving.

"Blue shirt or red shirt?"

"Brush teeth first or pajamas first?"

Practice New Routines Slowly

New routines may take time to learn.

Practice during calm moments, use visual support, and repeat the same steps until the routine becomes more familiar.

Celebrate Independence

Independence develops one step at a time.

Celebrate when a child completes part of a routine, follows a visual schedule, transitions successfully, or tries a new step independently.

PREDICTABILITY CAN CREATE SECURITY

Routines Are Tools, Not Rules

Routines can provide structure and comfort, but flexibility matters too. The goal is not perfection. The goal is to help children understand their day, feel prepared, and gradually build confidence and independence.
